Meyer’s VIC (Voltage Intensifier Circuit) transformer was typically housed in a CNC-machined aircraft-grade aluminum box for a few key reasons: 1.

Heat Dissipation Aircraft Aluminum: The aluminum casing helps dissipate heat generated by high-frequency, high-voltage pulses in the transformer.

Aircraft-grade aluminum has excellent thermal conductivity, which helps keep the VIC cool during operation.

CNC Machining: Precision machining ensures a tight fit and uniform enclosure, optimizing heat dissipation and durability. 2.

Shielding from Electromagnetic Interference (EMI) High-voltage pulsing circuits like the VIC can generate significant EMI, which could interfere with nearby electronic components or sensors.

The aluminum casing acts as a Faraday cage, helping to contain electromagnetic radiation within the enclosure and shielding sensitive components.

Enhanced Stability: By reducing interference, the aluminum box helps the VIC maintain consistent, stable operation, which is critical for producing the precise pulse frequencies needed for water fuel splitting. 3.

Protection from Environmental Factors The aluminum box offers protection from environmental elements such as moisture, dust, and physical impacts.

This is especially important if the VIC is used in a laboratory or field setting where it may be exposed to harsh conditions. 4.
